Questions, answered.

Why does it need Accessibility and Automation permissions?

Accessibility lets MouseGestures watch cursor position and modifier keys system-wide (that’s how gestures are detected) and move or resize other apps’ windows. Automation lets it drive system actions like volume and brightness. Both are granted once under System Settings → Privacy & Security, and everything is processed locally — nothing is recorded or sent anywhere.

Is it on the Mac App Store?

No — and it can’t be. System-wide gesture detection and window control require Accessibility APIs that the App Store sandbox forbids. MouseGestures ships as a Developer ID–signed, Apple-notarized DMG instead, so it installs cleanly without Gatekeeper warnings.

What happens when the 30-day trial ends?

The app keeps working — it simply drops to the Free tier: core actions and a single profile. Your gesture configuration is preserved, and entering a Pro license key at any point unlocks everything again.

Do I need an account or a subscription?

Neither. Pro is a one-time $9.99 license key that’s validated entirely offline on your Mac. No account, no server check-ins, no recurring charges.

Does it run on Intel Macs?

MouseGestures targets Apple silicon (arm64) on macOS 13 Ventura or later. Intel Macs aren’t supported.

Will it conflict with Rectangle, Raycast, or BetterTouchTool?

They coexist fine. MouseGestures only acts when one of its configured triggers fires — a zone-plus-modifier combination, a mapped mouse button, or a hotkey — so as long as your triggers don’t overlap with another tool’s, they stay out of each other’s way.
