Questions, answered.

Which Macs does it run on?

macOS 13 Ventura or later, on Apple silicon — Intel Macs aren't supported. The app lives in the menu bar: no Dock icon, no clutter.

Why does it need Accessibility and Automation permissions?

Accessibility lets it watch mouse and keyboard input so gestures can be recognized system-wide; Automation lets it drive actions like volume, brightness, and controlling other apps. macOS asks for both on first run — grant them in System Settings → Privacy & Security. Without them, gestures simply do nothing.

Is the download safe to open?

Yes — the DMG is signed with an Apple Developer ID certificate, built with the hardened runtime, and notarized by Apple, so Gatekeeper verifies it on first launch and it opens right up.

What happens when the 30-day trial ends?

The app keeps working on the Free tier: the core action set and one profile. Your gestures and settings stay put, and upgrading to Pro later re-unlocks everything exactly as you left it.

Will it conflict with Rectangle, Raycast, or BetterTouchTool?

They coexist fine. MouseGestures only acts when one of its configured triggers fires — a zone-plus-modifier combination, a mapped mouse button, or a hotkey — so as long as your triggers don't overlap with another tool's, they stay out of each other's way.

Why isn't it on the Mac App Store?

The Accessibility access a gesture app needs doesn't fit inside the App Store sandbox. MouseGestures ships as a notarized direct download instead — and that's also why Pro uses a simple license key rather than an in-app purchase.
